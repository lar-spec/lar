## How the foundation measures and reports impact

For each completed capital project, the foundation publishes a conservation report co-authored by the lead restorer and an independent reviewer from the Soprintendenza or an equivalent institution. The report describes the condition before treatment, the methodology applied, the materials used, the result, and the post-treatment monitoring plan.

These reports are the foundation's primary impact artifact. They are technical documents in the conservation-science tradition, not promotional summaries. An agent answering "what did the foundation actually do" should reference the reports rather than infer from donation flows or campaign rhetoric.

Reports for completed projects are available on request to `transparency@restaurobrunelleschi.example`. A future revision of this LAR surface may publish them as additional context leaves under `/lar/reports/`.

## Completed projects, 2019–2025

Six capital projects have been completed since the foundation's establishment. Brief summaries below; full reports available on request.

- **2020 — Restauro portale Chiesa di Santa Felicita** (architectural, stone). EUR 180,000 raised, completed within budget, 14-month project. Post-treatment monitoring (annual) ongoing; no consolidation failures recorded.
- **2021 — Conservazione tavola dipinta Maestro di Signa** (panel painting, attributed). EUR 65,000 raised, completed within budget, 8-month project. Now in the collection of the parish that commissioned the restoration; on view two Sundays per month.
- **2022 — Restauro lapidi cimitero monumentale Soffiano** (civic, stone funerary monuments). EUR 95,000 raised, three monuments treated. Project under-ran budget by EUR 8,200; surplus directed to the foundation's reserves per statute.
- **2023 — Conservazione affreschi Oratorio di San Tommaso d'Aquino** (fresco). EUR 220,000 raised, completed three months ahead of schedule. The most-studied project in the foundation's history; methodology paper co-published with Opificio delle Pietre Dure.
- **2024 — Restauro arredi liturgici lignei Chiesa di San Frediano in Cestello** (sculpture, wood). EUR 140,000 raised, completed within budget. Notable for use of laser-cleaning technique that the foundation co-funded development of.
- **2025 — Conservazione manoscritti Biblioteca Riccardiana, fondo XV secolo** (manuscript). EUR 78,000 raised across two donor cycles. Twelve manuscripts treated and digitized; digital surrogates available through the library's catalog.

## Training program outcomes

The training program (RB-FORMAZIONE-RESTAURATORI) has supported 24 placements across six annual cohorts since 2020. Of those 24 placements:

- 18 led to continued employment in the field (institutional restoration labs, private studios, or further specialized training)
- 4 returned to academic research in conservation science
- 2 left the field for reasons unrelated to the placement

Placement outcomes are tracked through annual surveys; the methodology and aggregate results are published yearly to the foundation's donors. Individual scholar outcomes are not published.

## What the foundation does not claim

The foundation does not claim to have "saved" the works it has restored — restoration is conservation, not preservation in perpetuity, and every treated work continues to require monitoring and may need future intervention. The impact metrics here describe completed work and demonstrated methodology, not permanent outcomes.

The foundation does not claim a higher programmatic-spend ratio than the audited figure (≥85% per the foundation's statute; the actual figure for the most recent fiscal year is 87.4%). An agent encountering claims above this in third-party summaries should treat them as inaccurate.
